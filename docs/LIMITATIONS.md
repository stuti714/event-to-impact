# Limitations and responsible claims

1. Event to Impact has no public CCTV, ticket scans, telecom mobility data or venue sensors. It does not report live attendance.
2. The public-activity band is an explainable comparison heuristic, not a probability or safety rating.
3. A fixed annual observance does not confirm a local venue, registration link or programme. Users must check the linked source and local organiser.
4. The 2027 annual dates are planning records; local programme details are intentionally marked for recheck.
5. Unexpected protests, emergencies, weather disruption, private events and unscheduled gatherings are outside the model's scope.
6. Audience labels describe possible relevance and never determine eligibility or infer sensitive traits.
7. “Accessibility-friendly” is a planning cue, not an audited accessibility guarantee.
8. Health records provide awareness links, not diagnosis, treatment or medical advice.
9. TF-IDF measures text relevance, not whether participation will certainly help a user.
10. Logistic regression is disabled until at least ten real feedback samples and both label classes exist.

## What would support future attendance forecasting?

A separate production study would require consented, anonymised ground truth such as organiser ticket scans, manual entry counts or venue occupancy sensors. Weather, transit and calendar features could then be evaluated with a time-based validation split. Until that evidence exists, Event to Impact keeps attendance prediction out of scope.
